ImageMagick's TIFF decoder (exercised by a libFuzzer harness that decodes TIFF input through the Magick++ encoder-fuzz driver, ASan build): decoding a stripped image (its data organized in TIFF strips rather than tiles) triggers a one-byte heap-buffer-overflow read while the reader converts a decoded strip's raw bytes into the image's per-pixel RGBA channels. Root cause: right before handing a strip's raw data to ImageMagick's generic sample-unpacking routine, the strip-decode path allocates its scratch buffer sized as the strip's exact packed byte count from libtiff plus a small, fixed slack margin meant to absorb the unpacking routine's read granularity — but that margin is sized for a narrower word than the routine actually consumes when the image's channel samples are processed at full 32-bit precision. The unpacking routine walks the buffer reading each of the four RGBA components as one complete machine word at a time, without checking how much genuine (non-padding) strip data remains, so once it reaches the final row's components the last word-sized read runs past the end of the undersized allocation. An equivalent allocation feeding the tiled-TIFF decode path a little further down in the same reader carries the identical undersized margin and is widened in the same change, although it is not what this particular crash exercises.
